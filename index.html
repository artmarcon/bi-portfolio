01 / Cases

Each case below is a pattern I deliver for commercial teams. Client data stays private, so the charts use anonymized sample data built with the same structure and logic as the real reports.

Sales analytics

Sales performance and decision intelligence

Problem
Plenty of ERP sales data, no single source of truth. KPIs were undefined and leadership decided on instinct.
What I built
A star schema on top of the ERP, a measure layer in DAX for revenue, ticket and growth (month over month and year over year), and a seller ranking with drill-through to customer level.
Result
One report the sales leadership opens every week, with underperforming segments and concentration risks visible on the first page.

Customer intelligence

RFV segmentation

Problem
Every customer got the same treatment. High-value accounts and dormant ones received the same attention from the sales team.
What I built
A Recency, Frequency and Value score computed in DAX from purchase history, classifying every customer into a segment with a clear commercial action, refreshed automatically.
Result
Sales effort allocated by segment value, with at-risk customers flagged before they go quiet. The same model fed a six-month commercial program built on segment missions.

Retention analytics

Cohort retention

Problem
Nobody could answer when customers stop buying, or which acquisition months produced the healthiest customers.
What I built
Cohorts anchored to each customer's first purchase and a retention matrix rendered as a heatmap, readable by non-technical stakeholders in seconds.
Result
The second and third month showed up as the main churn window, which moved retention actions to right after onboarding.

Revenue strategy

ABC and Pareto concentration

Problem
Revenue distribution was opaque. The business did not know how exposed it was to losing a handful of key accounts.
What I built
An ABC classification of customers and products by revenue contribution, with a cumulative Pareto curve and dynamic class thresholds.
Result
Confirmed a heavy dependency on a small Class A tier, and pointed to growth room in Class B accounts that were under-served.
